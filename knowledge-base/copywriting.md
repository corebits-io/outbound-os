# Copywriting — what actually makes cold copy get replies

Distilled from the best public, data-backed sources (not scraped emails). Use the **frameworks**, never paste the examples.

## The data (non-negotiable)
- **Short wins.** Aim **25–50 words** for email 1. Under 50 words gets ~60% more replies than 125+ (Lavender, across 200k+ emails). Over ~75 words → cut.
- **Ask a question, not a CTA.** Questions get ~**2× the replies** of statements. "Worth a quick look?" beats "book a 30-min demo." "Interested?" beats "let me know."
- **One idea, one ask** per email. Always.

## The structure — Josh Braun's 4-T
1. **Trigger** — a *specific, true* observation about THEM in line 1 (the hook). "Saw DeepEval crossed 10k stars," not "I see you're an AI company."
2. **Think** — surface the problem the trigger implies; **educate on the problem, not your solution.** Let them persuade themselves.
3. **Third-party credibility** — light, true proof (a peer / a result). Skip if you can't back it.
4. **Talk** — one low-friction question. Give **permission to say no** ("feel free to say no, but…").

## The anti-AI rubric (score EVERY draft; rewrite anything that fails)
- [ ] One **specific, true** observation in line 1 — could *only* be sent to this company?
- [ ] **Under ~75 words** (ideally 25–50)?
- [ ] **One idea + one question** (no second ask, no calendar link in email 1)?
- [ ] More **"you" than "I"** (Braun: ~8:2)?
- [ ] **Problem-first**, not pitch-first?
- [ ] **Permission to say no / zero pressure**?
- [ ] Reads like a human typed it — contractions, one idea per line, **no neat tricolon rhythm**?
- [ ] **No fabricated** results, numbers, or "it worked" claims?

## Banned phrases (instant AI tell — never use)
"hope this finds you well" · "I wanted to reach out" · "fellow founder" · "excited to" · "circling back" / "just following up" · "synergy" / "leverage" / "game-changer" / "revolutionary" · "at [Company], we…" · any neat 3-part parallel list.

## Patterns (illustrations — adapt, never copy verbatim)
- **Trigger → problem → question:** "Saw {{trigger}}. Usually that means {{problem}}. Worth a look?"
- **Permission-to-no:** "Feel free to say no — is {{problem}} on your radar this quarter?"
- **Peer proof:** "{{peer}} had the same {{problem}} — happy to share what worked."
