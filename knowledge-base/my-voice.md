# Your voice (from your real winners)

Distilled from the operator's top-performing campaigns (~7–8% per-lead reply rates). This is *your* style — `write-copy` should match it. (Generalized — your real campaigns + numbers stay private.)

## The pattern
- **Plain and short.** 5–7 short lines. No adjectives, no hype, no "hope this finds you well."
- **Open with a specific relevance reason, plainly:** "Saw you've been active in [their space], so I thought this might be relevant." / "Wanted to send a note since [firm] is active in [area]." / "The reason I'm reaching out is [specific link to them]."
- **State credibility flatly and fast** — who you are + ONE proof point with a **real number**: "[who you are] — [one true number / named win]." Facts, not bragging.
- **Lead with concrete specifics, not claims:** real numbers + named proof ("[$X] across [N] states," "[a project for a named customer]"). Proof beats adjectives.
- **Soft, specific ask — a question:** "Worth a 15-min call?" / "Want me to send the summary?" / "just reply back?" Low friction.
- **Follow-ups add ONE new angle each** (a different proof/benefit), never "just bumping." Subjects: short, number-led; follow-ups use "re:".
- Optional **P.S.** with a credibility nugget.

## What this voice does NOT do
- No chatty/clever lines or cute hooks ("I'm a founder too," "YC drills it into you").
- No fluff, no multi-asks, no calendar links in email 1, no fabricated numbers.

## The skeleton
1. **Relevance reason** — specific to them (the trigger).
2. **Who you are + 1 proof point with a number.**
3. **What you're doing / what you want** — one line.
4. **Soft, specific ask** — a question.

> Pair with `copywriting.md` (the rubric) and the per-campaign `winning-emails.md`. The trigger in step 1 comes from real research (`find-signals`) — that's what keeps it from sounding generic. Fill in your *own* real numbers/proof — don't borrow another campaign's.
